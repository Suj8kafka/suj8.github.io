Current welcome behavior supersedes the intro descriptions below: first desktop visits run for six seconds ending with animated Namaste; returning desktop visits show Namaste only. Phones retain their five-second sequence. See [loading-screen behavior](loading-screen.md).

GSAP 3.15.1 and ScrollTrigger are served from `assets/vendor/gsap/`. The small `assets/js/enhancements.js` scheduler waits for page load, full stylesheet activation, portrait decoding, loader completion, two paint opportunities and idle time. Desktop then downloads GSAP, its plugin and `motion.js`; compact screens wait until the first scroll (including a restored scroll position). Live presence and availability load in the background phase independently of that scroll. Core themes, dialogs and native anchor navigation remain available independently. A failed optional enhancement does not block the others.

The hero has generated inline critical CSS. `page-styles.js` starts downloading the full sheet after portrait decoding, then activates it after two paint opportunities. Reader interaction and direct section links prioritize the full layout immediately. On phones, secondary fonts wait until the first scroll; only the heading font is preloaded. See `docs/mobile-rendering.md` for the follow-up Lighthouse measurements and fallback checks.

Phones now show the requested five-second language-change welcome, ending in handwritten Namaste with orbital motion graphics. It can be skipped and bypasses reduced motion, save-data and direct section links. Ordinary desktop visits remain immediate; `/?intro=1` retains the desktop preview. See `docs/loading-screen.md`. The initial portrait is responsive WebP; only fine-pointer desktop devices with normal motion and sufficient resources fetch and parse the original SVG for its colour interaction, after initial content is visible. Native anchor scrolling and speed-responsive viewport blur retain their existing implementations.

Phone navigation is a fixed bottom bar with five labelled icons and an active-section indicator, including landscape on coarse-pointer screens up to 1100px. Safe-area and page-bottom padding protect content and touch targets. The top wordmark scrolls normally on phones; the desktop header keeps its existing behavior. See `docs/mobile-navigation.md`.

Hero and section entrance fades are disabled. Previously, delayed GSAP `from` tweens could hide content after it had already painted, producing a visible flash after loader dismissal or intersection. Text, portraits and cards now remain visible throughout startup and scrolling. Skill card colours are present in CSS from the first paint, with no intersection observer or timed colour resets. GSAP's media-query context still reverts depth styles and triggers when reduced motion or the layout changes.

`parallax.js` uses cached ScrollTrigger ranges for bounded hero, landscape, project-image and decorative-illustration depth. It no longer measures every layer in a custom animation loop. Pointer movement reuses `quickTo` tweens on numeric state, with no pointer tilt on touch, compact or constrained devices. Reduced motion creates no parallax. Anchor navigation and hidden tabs pause owned depth triggers; arrival resumes them without resetting transforms.

Slower devices use shorter depth travel and no pointer animation. GSAP failure leaves static content readable. The JavaScript does not add scroll smoothing, pinning or transform wrappers around the document, so fixed navigation, live cursors and the viewport blur keep their existing behavior.

`scroll-trail.js` draws a broad accent-colour ribbon through the page, with long S-curves sweeping across the background. The stroke is 32–64px on desktop and 24px on mobile, with a low-opacity tint behind the content. The rounded leading edge advances as the reader scrolls, leaving the ribbon drawn from the beginning; scrolling back retracts it. It replaces the narrow gutter trail and separate dot. GSAP ScrollTrigger scrubs only the decorative SVG; it never hides, pins or transforms content. No additional GSAP plugin, scroll listener or perpetual animation loop is used.

The SVG is rendered at the current scroll position before it becomes visible. Geometry uses the main element's width and height and is measured on refresh, including viewport and content-size changes, rather than during each scroll frame. Sections sit above the ribbon; fixed dialogs retain their existing stacking order. Reduced motion creates no ribbon, hidden tabs pause its trigger, and teardown removes the SVG, trigger and observers.
